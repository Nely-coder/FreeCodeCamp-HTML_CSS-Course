INTRODUCTION

The game of chess finds its roots of origin in India where it was first called Chaturanga. And it has now evolved to the current form that it is today. Chess is a board game for two players, played on a square board consisting of 64 squares arranged in an 8×8 grid.The players, referred to as "White" and "Black", each control sixteen pieces: one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights, and eight pawns, with each piece type having a different pattern of movement.

An enemy piece may be captured (removed from the board) by moving one's own piece onto the square it occupies. The object of the game is to "checkmate" (threaten with inescapable capture) the enemy king. There are also several ways a game can end in a draw. Six unique pieces are used in the play of this strategy game. They are namely:

  • Pawn
  • Knight
  • Bishop
  • Rook
  • Queen
  • King
PAWN

This is the bald headed short piece on the board.

It only moves one step forward directionally and captures diagonally.

KNIGHT

This is the horse-like piece on the board.

It moves in an L-like shape and can jump over other pieces.

BISHOP

This piece has a slit in its slightly rounded top head.

Directionally, the bishop is only able to move diagonally.

ROOK

This piece has a slit in its slightly rounded top head.

It may move any number of squares horizontally or vertically without jumping, and it may capture an enemy piece on its path

QUEEN

The queen is traditionally the second tallest piece in a chess set after the king. It is distinguished by a stylized crown or coronet with multiple points or bumps, often topped with an orb in the center.

The queen is the most powerful piece in the game of chess. It can move any number of squares vertically, horizontally or diagonally, combining the powers of the rook and bishop

KING

In traditional Staunton chess sets, the king's crown is uniquely adorned with a small Christian cross finial at its very summit.

The king is the most important piece in the game of chess. It may move to any adjacent square that is not controlled by an enemy piece.
